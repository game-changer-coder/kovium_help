# The option catalogue

Every choice second brain offers, in four lines (what it is, whether you need it, what it costs, what is recommended) and what changing it later costs.

## Token limit for adding documents

- What it is: The most of your subscription that adding documents may use each time, without asking you again.
- Do you need it: Everyone needs one, because adding documents is the main thing that uses your subscription.
- What it costs: It costs nothing itself; it only stops a run that would use more tokens than you allowed.
- What is recommended: Say yes to the figure shown with your first plan, or start with a few documents to see the real cost.
- Changing it later: You can raise it, lower it or remove it at any time, and nothing already added is affected.

## Asking before anything is spent

- What it is: Whether anything that uses tokens needs a plan you were shown first, however the work is asked for.
- Do you need it: Keep it on; it matters for programs that use second brain directly, since this conversation always shows the plan first.
- What it costs: It uses no tokens, and each piece of work starts with its plan, which is free.
- What is recommended: Keep it on.
- Changing it later: You can turn it on or off at any time, and nothing already added is affected.

## Better PDF reader

- What it is: A program on your computer that reads PDFs with columns, tables and formulas much better than the plain reader.
- Do you need it: You need it if your PDFs have tables, columns or equations, and the plan checks your own files for them.
- What it costs: It is a download of about 2 GB once, it is slower for each PDF, and it uses no tokens.
- What is recommended: Install it before the first run if your PDFs have tables or columns, and it turns itself on once it is installed.
- Changing it later: You can add it later, but documents already read keep the plain result, and reading them again costs time and tokens.

## Reader for Word files, web pages and e-books

- What it is: A small program that turns Word files, web pages and e-books into text second brain can read.
- Do you need it: You need it only if you add those kinds of files, and the plan checks your files for them.
- What it costs: It is a small download once, it takes a few seconds for each file, and it uses no tokens.
- What is recommended: Install it when your files include these kinds; otherwise skip it.
- Changing it later: Those files are left out until it is installed, and adding them afterwards works as normal.

## Equations in PDFs

- What it is: Reading the equations in your PDFs, so they appear as text and not as marked gaps.
- Do you need it: You need it if your documents hold many equations, and the plan looks for them in your files.
- What it costs: It uses no tokens when your computer reads them, but it can take minutes a page without a fast video card.
- What is recommended: Leave reading during conversion off, and let the nightly reading do it; you can ask for any document at once.
- Changing it later: Unread equations stay marked until they are read, and any document can have them read later without being added again.

## Reading scanned pages

- What it is: Reading the text out of scanned pages that are only pictures.
- Do you need it: You need it only if some of your files are scans, and the plan checks each file for text.
- What it costs: It is slow, it needs a download of its own, and it uses no tokens because your computer does the reading.
- What is recommended: Leave it on: only files with no text are read this way, and only once the better PDF reader is installed.
- Changing it later: Scans are read as soon as it is installed, and you add them as usual.

## Reading pictures

- What it is: Having the AI describe the figures and pictures inside your documents, so questions about them can be answered.
- Do you need it: You need it if the meaning of your documents sits in figures, and the plan counts the pictures it finds.
- What it costs: It uses tokens of your subscription for every picture it reads, up to a limit for each document.
- What is recommended: Leave it off, and turn it on for chosen documents once you have seen the estimate.
- Changing it later: You can have pictures read later for chosen documents without adding them again, and each one then costs tokens.

## Pictures on web pages

- What it is: Whether the pictures on web pages you add are downloaded and kept with the page.
- Do you need it: You need it only if you add web pages, and the plan checks your list for links.
- What it costs: It uses a little disk space and download time for each picture, and no tokens unless the pictures are read.
- What is recommended: Keep the default, which downloads pictures only for pages you add by their link.
- Changing it later: You can change it at any time, and it applies to pages added afterwards.

## Repairing lost letters

- What it is: Putting back letters that a PDF reader lost, such as two joined letters that came out as a question mark.
- Do you need it: Many PDFs have this problem, and second brain checks each document for it.
- What it costs: It is free: no tokens, no extra disk and only a moment for each document.
- What is recommended: Keep it on, because it changes a word only when the same document holds the correct spelling.
- Changing it later: You can turn it on at any time, and the nightly tidy repairs documents already added.

## Videos added from a link

- What it is: How videos are added from their link: from the captions the video site shows, never from the video file.
- Do you need it: You need it only if you add video links, and the plan checks your list for them.
- What it costs: It means a short wait between requests to the video site, and tokens as for any other document.
- What is recommended: Keep captions only, and let a video with no captions be refused rather than guessed.
- Changing it later: You can change it at any time, and it applies to videos added afterwards.

## Splitting long documents

- What it is: How a long document is cut into parts: by its own outline and contents page first, and by the AI only when those fail.
- Do you need it: You need it if you add books or documents of over a hundred pages, and the plan counts them.
- What it costs: The outline steps are free, and the AI look uses a few tokens for each book, inside your limit.
- What is recommended: Keep the defaults; a book that cannot be cut with confidence is read whole instead.
- Changing it later: A change applies to documents added afterwards; a book already added stays as it was cut.

## Other versions of one document

- What it is: What happens to a second version of a document you already have: kept and linked, kept apart, or held back.
- Do you need it: You need it if you keep drafts, editions or preprints, and second brain finds versions by their content.
- What it costs: Finding versions is free, and keeping one costs the same tokens as adding any other document.
- What is recommended: Keep versions and link them, so that nothing is lost and answers can name both.
- Changing it later: You can remove a version, or only its link, at any time through review.

## Keeping copies of your originals

- What it is: Whether second brain keeps its own copy of each original file, or only points to where the file already lies.
- Do you need it: Keep copies unless your files are a large library you do not want twice, and the plan checks where your files are.
- What it costs: Copies use as much disk as your originals, pointing uses none but needs the files to stay put, and neither uses tokens.
- What is recommended: Keep copies, so that the collection can be moved or backed up as one thing.
- Changing it later: You can change it, but a change does not move files already added, so an older document keeps the way it was saved.

## Named folders for your originals

- What it is: Giving a short name to a folder that holds your originals, so notes point to the name and not to a full address.
- Do you need it: You need it when your originals live in a folder you keep elsewhere or share between computers.
- What it costs: It costs nothing in time, disk or tokens; it is one line.
- What is recommended: Name the folder once when you give the collection its files, and the notes then survive a move.
- Changing it later: You can point the name at a new place at any time, for example on another computer.

## Kind of note when none is clear

- What it is: The kind of note a document gets when nothing in the document says what kind it is.
- Do you need it: You need it only if your documents are mostly of one kind.
- What it costs: It costs nothing; it is one word, used only when a file's own look is not clear.
- What is recommended: Leave it empty, so that it follows the kind your collection holds most of.
- Changing it later: You can change it at any time, and it applies to documents added afterwards.

## Renamed or moved originals

- What it is: What happens when you rename or move an original: the notes are fixed by themselves, or each fix is first offered to you.
- Do you need it: Anyone who reorganises files needs it, and second brain recognises a moved file by its content.
- What it costs: It is free, with no tokens and only a few seconds each night.
- What is recommended: Keep fixing exact matches by themselves; likely matches are always offered to you first.
- Changing it later: You can change it at any time, and every fix can be undone.

## Search by meaning

- What it is: Finding passages by what they mean, so that a question can match a note that words the same idea differently.
- Do you need it: Almost every collection gains from it, and it needs a small free program running on your computer.
- What it costs: It is a small download once, with some disk space for its index and a moment for each question, and it uses no tokens.
- What is recommended: Keep it on, and install the free program the first time the plan says it is missing.
- Changing it later: You can install it at any time, and notes added in the meantime are caught up by the nightly tidy.

## Matching your wording

- What it is: Matching your question to the labels your collection already uses, so that different wording still finds the right notes.
- Do you need it: It helps when you and your documents word things differently, and it matters more as the collection grows.
- What it costs: It uses a small AI call for each new question, a little of your subscription, and a repeated question is free.
- What is recommended: Keep it on; it is small, and the answer is saved after the first time.
- Changing it later: You can turn it off or on at any time, and nothing already added is affected.

## Remembering written answers

- What it is: Keeping written answers, so that the same question on unchanged notes is answered again without the AI.
- Do you need it: It only matters if you ask the same thing more than once.
- What it costs: It uses a little disk space and no tokens, and it saves tokens on repeated questions.
- What is recommended: Keep it on.
- Changing it later: You can turn it off or on at any time, and saved answers older than a month are dropped.

## A fuller look for thin answers

- Do you need it: It helps when answers come out thin, and it only matters if you ask for written answers.

## Nightly tidy

- Changing it later: You can choose another level at any time, and the free steps of the night run either way.
